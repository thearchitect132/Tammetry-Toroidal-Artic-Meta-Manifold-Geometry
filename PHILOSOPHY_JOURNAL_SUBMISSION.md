## PHILOSOPHY VERSION: KEY SECTIONS

### Abstract (Philosophy Focus)

Tammetry provides a new epistemological framework that unifies human knowledge across all domains. We propose that all knowing organizes into five domains (Identity, Structure, Restoration, Direction, Expansion), operates across five horizons (Forward, Return, Divergent, Convergent, Hidden), and develops at five recursion depths (Concrete, Abstract, Meta-theoretical, Civilisational, Tammetric). This creates a recursive toroidal knowledge geometry where disciplines are not isolated but interconnected points in five-dimensional domain-space. We show how this framework resolves classical epistemological problems: the fragmentation of knowledge, the incommensurability of paradigms, and the difficulty of interdisciplinary synthesis. We argue that Tammetric epistemology provides both descriptive power (explaining how knowledge actually develops) and prescriptive guidance (suggesting how knowledge should organize for coherence and utility). The framework has implications for education, science policy, interdisciplinary research, and civilization-scale knowledge management.

**Keywords:** epistemology, knowledge organization, domain theory, interdisciplinary synthesis, second-order thinking, recursive knowledge structures

### Philosophical Contributions

#### 1. New Epistemological Architecture
- Five domains as fundamental knowing structures
- Horizons as epistemic orientations
- Recursion as knowledge depth
- Replaces linear category-based taxonomies with recursive toroidal structure

#### 2. Resolution of Classical Problems

**Problem 1: Fragmentation of Knowledge**
- Classical: Each discipline isolated, no unifying principle
- Tammetry: All disciplines on single knowledge torus, naturally connected

**Problem 2: Kuhnian Incommensurability**
- Classical: Different paradigms are incommensurable (can't be compared)
- Tammetry: Paradigms differ in horizon/domain/depth but share underlying structure

**Problem 3: Reductionism vs. Holism**
- Classical: Must choose between explaining parts or whole
- Tammetry: Recursive structure explains both simultaneously at different depths

#### 3. Epistemological Unification

| Classical Philosophy | Tammetric Framework |
|---|---|
| Essence (metaphysics) → Accident (epistemology) | Identity + Structure (unified) |
| Subject (Descartes) → Object (Kant) | Directional + Structural domains (unified) |
| Determinism vs. Randomness | Deterministic at domain level, random at boundary |
| Meaning (semiotics) → Reference (logic) | Expansion + Direction (both in domain space) |

### Key Philosophical Arguments

#### Argument 1: Domain Primacy
**Premise 1:** All human endeavors (science, art, ethics, etc.) are fundamentally organized by concerns with Identity, Structure, Restoration, Direction, or Expansion.

**Premise 2:** These concerns are universally recognizable across all cultures and historical periods.

**Premise 3:** They are independent and irreducible to each other.

**Conclusion:** The five domains are fundamental epistemological structures, not culturally contingent categories.

#### Argument 2: Horizon Realism
**Premise 1:** Orientation (forward, return, etc.) shapes knowledge development.

**Premise 2:** Horizon shifts are observable in disciplinary history (e.g., physics from return-oriented to forward-oriented).

**Premise 3:** Horizons determine which problems are visible and which are ignored.

**Conclusion:** Horizons are real epistemological features, not mere metaphors.

#### Argument 3: Recursive Coherence
**Premise 1:** Knowledge at one recursion depth produces knowledge at the next depth (meta-knowledge).

**Premise 2:** This recursive process is infinite and self-similar.

**Premise 3:** The only stable structure for infinite self-similar recursion is toroidal.

**Conclusion:** Knowledge geometry is fundamentally toroidal.

### Objections and Responses

**Objection 1:** "Aren't the five domains just another classification system?"

**Response:** No. They're not categories imposed externally but fundamental structures that emerge from analysis of what all knowledge systems actually do.

**Objection 2:** "How do you know these five are sufficient?"

**Response:** Proof: any discipline's core concerns map to one of the five. Counter-example would falsify the claim.

**Objection 3:** "This seems too neat. Doesn't it oversimplify?"

**Response:** At one level, yes (all knowledge into five domains). At another level, no (recursive depth and domain coupling allow infinite complexity).
